# Cognitive reflection task

Seven adapted items: four based on Toplak, West, & Stanovich (2014), followed by three based on Frederick (2005). Text is available in Japanese, English, and Simplified Chinese, selected by settings.LANGUAGE_CODE.

- Frederick (2005), Cognitive Reflection and Decision Making: https://doi.org/10.1257/089533005775196732
- Toplak et al. (2014), Assessing miserly information processing: An expansion of the Cognitive Reflection Test: https://doi.org/10.1080/13546783.2013.844729

These are adapted translations, not validated standard language versions. Names, scenarios, and amounts differ from the original instruments. All monetary scenarios use yen, not exchange-rate conversions. The bat and ball cost 5,500 yen together, with a 5,000-yen price difference (ball: 250 yen). Company transactions are 80, 90, 95, and 110 million yen (profit: 25 million yen); the stock investment starts at 1 million yen.

Each question allows 60 seconds and pays 20 yen for a correct answer (maximum 140 yen). These timing and payment rules are project-specific. Answers, in order: 4, 35, 25, C, 250, 5, 47. Only question 4 is multiple choice. Results are disclosed after the questionnaire.

## Investment decision

After question 7, participants allocate an additional 100 yen between a safe balance and a risky investment. Investment is in increments of 1 yen. Successful odd investments produce half-yen earnings, which are retained in payoff calculations. With probability 0.5 the risky investment returns 2.5 times the principal; otherwise it returns zero. The uninvested balance is retained. This untimed decision is not scored as a CRT item. It pays 0–250 yen separately from the CRT reward, with the outcome revealed only on the final payoff page. The draw is stored once, and investment, outcome, and earnings are transferred through participant.vars.

Source: Charness & Gneezy, Portfolio Choice and Risk Attitudes: An Experiment (2010), https://faculty.econ.ucsb.edu/~charness/papers/portfolio.pdf. Yen denomination and investment increments are project adaptations.
